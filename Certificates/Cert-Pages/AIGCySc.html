Summary of the Course

This course provided insight into the work carried out by an Information Security Analyst at AIG, covering both the technical and non-technical sides of responding to a cyber threat. The course focused on analysing alerts from the Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and applying that intel to reduce the risk of an attack on the business. The module had me assume the role of a security analyst, tasked with notifying internal stakeholders who were at risk from a ransomware attack and then helping to recover hacked files using Python. The course was designed to showcase vulnerability management alongside the importance of clear communication during a live threat.

Learning Objectives

The primary focus of this course was based on the work completed by incident response and security teams, those were:

  • Analyse security alerts from CISA and research reported vulnerabilities to understand how they could affect a business.
  • Communicate technical risk clearly to internal stakeholders and provide actionable remediation advice.
  • Respond to a ransomware attack using Python and understand how weak passwords can be exploited.
  • Deepen knowledge of vulnerability management by identifying, analysing, and prioritising potential threats.

Work Produced & Problems Overcome

Task 1

For Task 1, I assumed the role of an Information Security Analyst, responsible for responding to a newly released CISA alert on a zero-day vulnerability in Apache Log4j. First, I reviewed recent CISA publications and researched the reported vulnerability to understand what it was, how it could be exploited, and why it posed such a serious risk. Next, I assessed how the vulnerability could affect the Product Development Staging Environment and which teams needed to be made aware. Lastly, I drafted an advisory email to the affected teams, alerting them of the vulnerability and explaining how to remediate it before it could be exploited. Communicating technical risk is just as important as understanding it, and this task was designed to strengthen the ability to turn complex threats into clear, actionable guidance for the people who need it.

Task 2

Task 2 saw a direct shift from communication to the technical side of a cyber attack. After a ransomware attack, I was tasked with recovering an encrypted file using Python. To do this, I wrote a brute-force algorithm and ran it against the RockYou wordlist, a real-world list of leaked passwords, until it eventually cracked the encryption. Seeing this first-hand showed how quickly weak and commonly used passwords can fall apart, and why strong password practices and layered defences matter so much. Together with Task 1, this showed the full picture of incident response, from alerting stakeholders to the technical side of the work.

Learning Outcomes

Throughout this course, I developed a strong understanding of the work carried out by Incident Responders at both a technical and executive level. Cybersecurity translation & communication is a vital tool for any professional. Learning how IR teams actually research and share flaws/exploits using CISA and the CVE system demonstrated the ecosystem of cybersecurity and blue team operations. The direct pivot to technical coding was a strong reality check that incident responders must handle personal communication alongside technical duties. Learning about brute-force algorithms and getting the rush when I finally cracked the encryption was a great experience. This course taught me not only about corporate translation & improving my coding skills, but also the split of duties that comes with the field and the range of work you do.
